#### I. PURPOSE AND SUMMARY

S. 4716, the Financial Management Risk Reduction Act, amends Section 7504 of title 31, United States Code, to improve single audit requirements and enhance oversight of federal funds. The Act establishes a regular government-wide analysis of single audit quality and directs the development of analytic tools and strategies to identify cross-governmental risks to federal award funds. The Act also requires agencies to identify recipients who should have undergone audits but did not and mandates biennial reports to Congress on these entities. #### II. BACKGROUND AND NEED FOR THE LEGISLATION

The Single Audit Act of 1984 requires federal grant recipients who receive more than $750,000 to report an independent audit of their internal financial controls annually to the federal government.1 The Single Audit Act sought to increase accountability while reducing burden on grant recipients by mandating a single, consolidated audit rather than audits on a grant-by-grant basis.2 In 2022, over 40,000 state, local, tribal, and territorial governments and nonprofits submitted single audits. This legislation addresses recommendations from the Government Accountability Office (GAO) to increase the usability and use of single audit information to reduce federal financial management risk.3

Single audits represent a potentially valuable resource for conducting oversight and stewardship of federal dollars. However, persistent audit quality and reliability problems have limited the effectiveness of single audits.4 The Office of Management and Budget’s (OMB) Uniform Grants Guidance requires each federal agency to conduct quality control reviews on its audits, and requires OMB to coordinate a governmentwide audit quality review every six years, or at an interval determined by the OMB Director.5 Despite this requirement, the last cross-government quality review was conducted in 2007, and it estimated that only 49% of all sampled audits were acceptable.6 Later analyses of single audit quality data conducted by non-governmental entities confirmed continuing challenges. A 2017 analysis of single audit peer reviews coordinated by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants found widespread nonconformity to professional standards among auditors who conduct single audits.7 Ultimately, these problems with audit quality and the lack of routine quality reviews led GAO to recommend that Congress codify the requirement for OMB to direct a cross-government quality review.8 This bill responds to that recommendation by codifying such a report.

The Single Audit Act also directs the Federal Audit Clearinghouse to review whether grant recipients should be submitting single audits but are not.9 However, GAO recommended that it would be more administratively efficient for agencies to track whether their grant recipients are properly submitting audits, in accordance with OMB guidance. Recent technological advances in managing audit records and developments in data science have created new possibilities for using existing single audit data to minimize financial risks. In 2023, the General Services Administration (GSA) took over hosting the single audit data from the Census Bureau, introducing new tools to validate and download existing data. Despite their ability to improve the usability of single audit data to mitigate risks to federal funds, these analytic tools are only currently available in the oversight community. Due to the success of these proof-of-concept pilot programs, GAO recommended that OMB and GSA create a strategy and analytic tools to mitigate risks to federal award funds.12 This Act responds to those recommendations by directing OMB to create a cross-government strategy on financial risk regarding single audits and directing GSA to create analytic tools to use single audit data more effectively.

#### III. LEGISLATIVE HISTORY

Senator Gary Peters (D–MI) introduced S. 4716, the Financial Management Risk Reduction Act, on July 11, 2024, with original cosponsor Senator Ron Johnson (R–WI). The bill was referred to the Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs.

The Committee considered S. 4716 at a business meeting on July 31, 2024. At the business meeting, Senators Peters and Johnson offered a substitute amendment, as well as a modification to that amendment. The Peters-Johnson substitute amendment, as modified, made technical edits to better align the cross-governmental quality review with GAO’s generally accepted government auditing standards. It also included language to indicate that no additional funds are authorized to be appropriated to carry out the Act. The Committee adopted the modification to the Peters-Johnson substitute amendment, and the Peters-Johnson substitute amendment, as modified, by unanimous consent, with Senators Peters, Carper, Hassan, Sinema, Rosen, Ossoff, Blumenthal, Butler, Paul, Lankford, and Scott present.

The bill, as amended by the Peters-Johnson substitute amendment, as modified, was ordered reported favorably by roll call vote 4

#### IV. SECTION-BY-SECTION ANALYSIS OF THE BILL, AS REPORTED

#### Section 1. Short title

This section establishes the short title of the bill as the Financial Management Risk Reduction Act.

#### Section 2. Single audit improvements

This section amends Section 7504 of title 31, United States Code, as follows:

Subsection (1) modifies Section 7504(a), to direct federal agencies to assist in the single audit data quality review by participating in and furnishing information for the review. It also requires each agency to identify federal award recipients that should have submitted a single audit but did not (recipients that expend $300,000 or more in federal awards during the recipient’s fiscal year but did not undergo an audit).

Subsection (2) makes changes to Section 7504(c), removing the responsibilities for the Federal Audit Clearinghouse to report on recipients who did not submit the required single audits. The bill directs these responsibilities to each relevant agency.

Subsection (3) adds four new subsections to Section 7504, as follows:

Section 7504(d) requires OMB within two years to submit a biennial report to the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs and the House Committee on Oversight and Accountability, listing the recipients identified under subsection (a) who did not submit the required audits.

Section 7504(e) directs OMB to designate one or more federal agencies to conduct a government-wide analysis of single audit quality. This analysis may consider reviews by federal agencies, Inspectors General, state auditors, and external peer reviews. The designated agencies must complete this analysis every six years, starting three years after the enactment of this bill. OMB must submit a summary of each review to the aforementioned congressional committees and make it publicly available.

Section 7504(f) requires OMB, in coordination with GSA, the council of Federal Financial Assistance, and key single audit liaisons of federal agencies, to develop a strategy within two years of enactment for using audit data to identify cross-governmental risks to federal award funds. It also directs GSA to develop analytic tools within two years of enactment to use audit data to identify cross-governmental risks to federal award funds.

Section 7504(g) directs the Comptroller General to evaluate, within four years of enactment, the effectiveness of the strategy and analytic tools developed, the reporting burdens on auditors and audited entities, the capacity of auditors and audited entities to ful-5

fill single audit requirements, and the responsiveness of federal agencies to repeat single audit findings and corrective action plans.

#### Section 3. No additional funds

This section specifies that no additional funds are authorized to be appropriated to carry out this Act or the amendments made by it.

#### VI. CONGRESSIONAL BUDGET OFFICE COST ESTIMATE

S. 4716 would amend the requirements for federal and nonfederal entities with respect to single audits. In general, current law requires that any nonfederal entity that spends more than a specified amount in federal grants, loans, or contracts within a fiscal year submit to an independent audit, called a single audit, and report the results to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse (FAC). Specifically, the bill would:

- Require the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) to designate at least one federal agency to conduct a governmentwide quality analysis of single audits,
- Require the General Services Administration (GSA) to develop analytical tools to identify risks related to the use of federal award funds, and 6

- Impose additional reporting requirements on federal agencies.

The bill also would require the Government Accountability Office to report, within four years of enactment, on single audits and GSA’s analytical tools.

The single audit is an oversight tool to ensure that an award recipient has adequate internal controls over federal programs and is complying with relevant requirements. The FAC reviews and stores single-audit reports and the underlying data. Beginning in 2023, GSA assumed the clearinghouse’s responsibilities from the Census Bureau. Since then, GSA has spent about $10 million updating the FAC; ongoing maintenance is funded under interagency agreements.

Because single audits are a key component of the oversight of federal funds, CBO expects that the agency OMB directs to complete analysis of single audits would delegate the work to its inspector general. Using information from OMB and GSA on the current FAC and data on the costs of similar analyses provided by inspectors general, CBO estimates that implementing S. 4716 would cost $30 million over the 2025–2029 period; any related spending would be subject to the availability of appropriated funds. Those costs would primarily be for GSA’s administrative and personnel costs, and for the additional costs incurred by the relevant inspector general.
